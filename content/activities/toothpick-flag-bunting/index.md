## What You'll Create

Time to celebrate — everything! 🎉 Your little party planners will cut tiny triangles from colourful **paper**, fold and glue them onto **toothpicks** to create adorable miniature flags, then thread them onto **string** to make the tiniest, most charming bunting you've ever seen. Use it to decorate a dolls' house, a sandcastle, a birthday cake, or string it across a bookshelf — instant party!

## Have fun!

- 🎂 Poke individual flags into cupcakes or a birthday cake for instant party decoration!
- 🏰 Create bunting for a toy castle, doll's house, or LEGO city!
- 🌍 Research real flags from different countries and make a world flag line!
- ✉️ Spell someone's name across the bunting as a personalised banner!

## Why It's Amazing

* **Fine Motor Precision:** Cutting tiny shapes and gluing small pieces develops exceptional finger control and hand-eye coordination. ✋

* **Pattern and Design:** Planning flag sequences, colour patterns, and decorative details exercises visual creativity and sequential thinking. 🎨

* **Scale and Proportion:** Working at miniature scale helps children understand relative size and develop spatial awareness. 📐

* **Celebration Culture:** Making party decorations teaches children that celebrations can be created, not just consumed — empowering and joyful! 🥳

## Pro Tips

For **ages 3–5**: Pre-cut the triangles and focus on gluing and decorating. Use a glue stick instead of PVA for less mess. Make larger flags (5–6 cm) for easier handling. The toothpick flagpoles are the exciting part — let them stick flags into playdough mountains!

For **ages 5–8**: Let them cut their own triangles and plan colour sequences. Introduce symmetry — can they make matching patterns on front and back? Challenge them to spell words across the bunting.

For **ages 8–12**: Research real flag designs — national flags, nautical signal flags, or heraldic banners. Create themed bunting sets for different occasions. Use the flags for cake decorating or miniature scene-building.

**Secret Pro Move**: For bunting that hangs beautifully, fold each paper flag over the string (instead of using toothpicks) and glue the two halves together with the string running through the fold. This gives a more traditional bunting look and the flags hang at a natural angle! 📐
