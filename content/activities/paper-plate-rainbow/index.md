## What You'll Create

Chase that rainbow! 🌈 Your little artists will turn half a **paper plate** into a stunning rainbow with flowing **tissue paper** streamers hanging below! Paint the plate with stripes of every colour in the rainbow, glue on fluffy cotton wool clouds, and attach colourful tissue paper streamers that flutter in the breeze. Hang it in a window and watch the colours glow!


## How to Set It Up

### Step 1: Cut and Paint the Plate

Cut a **paper plate** in half — you only need one half. With the curved side facing up, paint rainbow stripes across the plate using **poster paint** — red on the outside, then orange, yellow, green, blue, and purple toward the inside. Let each colour dry a little before adding the next. 🎨


### Step 2: Add Clouds

Pull apart **cotton wool** into fluffy cloud shapes. Use **PVA glue** to stick clouds at each end of the rainbow (on the flat edge). Press them on generously — the fluffier the better! These are your rain clouds. ☁️


### Step 3: Make Streamers

Cut **tissue paper** into long strips — one strip for each rainbow colour. Make them about 20cm long. Glue the top of each strip along the flat (straight) edge of the plate, in rainbow order. These will hang down as flowing streamers! ✂️


### Step 4: Hang and Display

Poke a small hole at the top of the rainbow and thread a piece of **string** through it. Tie a loop for hanging. Display your rainbow in a window, on a door handle, or from the ceiling. Watch the tissue paper streamers dance in the breeze! ✨


## Have fun!

- 🌤️ Make a sunshine to go with your rainbow — yellow plate with paper ray strips!
- 🎶 Sing "I Can Sing a Rainbow" while you craft!
- 📐 Learn the colour order — Richard Of York Gave Battle In Vain!
- 🌧️ Add raindrop shapes cut from blue paper falling from the clouds!


## Why It's Amazing

* **Colour Learning:** Painting rainbow stripes in order teaches children the visible light spectrum and colour sequencing — real science wrapped in art! 🔬

* **Fine Motor Skills:** Cutting strips, gluing small pieces, and painting neat stripes all develop the hand control needed for writing. ✋

* **Sensory Experience:** The combination of smooth paint, fluffy cotton wool, and crinkly tissue paper provides rich multi-texture sensory input. 🤲

* **Pride of Display:** A rainbow hanging in the window is a visible achievement that brings joy to the whole family — children feel genuine pride. 🏆


## Pro Tips

For **ages 3–5**: Pre-cut the plate and tissue paper strips. Let them paint the stripes (don't worry about neatness — messy rainbows are beautiful too!) and stick on cotton wool clouds. The sticking is the best part!

For **ages 5–8**: Let them cut their own strips and paint carefully. Challenge them to name all the rainbow colours in order. Can they blend colours where they meet for a smooth gradient effect?

For **ages 8–12**: Challenge them to create a double rainbow (two sets of stripes!). Experiment with tissue paper weaving or curling techniques for the streamers. Research why real rainbows form and what order the colours appear in.

**Secret Pro Move**: Lightly mist the painted plate with water from a spray bottle before it fully dries — the colours will bleed into each other slightly, creating a gorgeous natural gradient just like a real rainbow! 🎯
