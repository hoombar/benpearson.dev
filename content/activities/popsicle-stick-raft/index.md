## What You'll Create

Set sail! ⛵ Your young shipbuilders will construct a miniature raft from **popsicle sticks** lashed together with **string**, add a paper sail on a stick mast, and then test whether it actually floats! Launch it in a bowl of water and watch it bob on the surface. Will it hold a tiny passenger? How much cargo can it carry before it sinks? It's hands-on engineering that gets wet!

## Have fun!

- 🏋️ Test how many coins your raft can carry — keep a tally!
- 🌬️ Race rafts across a bath by blowing through straws!
- 🧸 Add a tiny toy passenger and build a cabin from more sticks!
- 📊 Compare raft designs — wide vs narrow, more sticks vs fewer!

## Why It's Amazing

* **Buoyancy Science:** Children learn why some things float and others sink — surface area, weight distribution, and displacement become tangible concepts. 🔬

* **Engineering Skills:** Building a raft that actually holds together in water requires planning, construction techniques, and iterative improvement. ⚙️

* **Problem-Solving:** When the first raft sinks or tips, children naturally investigate why and redesign — real engineering thinking in action. 🧠

* **Historical Connection:** Rafts are one of humanity's oldest inventions! Children connect with the same challenges ancient people faced crossing rivers and seas. 📖

## Pro Tips

For **ages 4–6**: Pre-glue the base and let it dry before the session. Let them help with the string lashing and focus on the exciting parts — decorating the sail and the float test! The suspense of "will it float?" is magical at this age.

For **ages 6–9**: Let them build from scratch. Encourage experimentation — what if you use more sticks? What if you add sides? Challenge them to carry the heaviest load possible. Keep a data table of results.

For **ages 9–12**: Introduce engineering constraints — use exactly 12 sticks and 30cm of string. Who can build the raft that carries the most weight? Research real raft-building techniques (log cabin style, outrigger design). Calculate the weight-to-area ratio.

**Secret Pro Move**: Rub the bottom of the raft lightly with a candle or wax crayon before launching — the thin wax coating makes the wood more water-resistant and your raft will float much longer without getting waterlogged! 🎯
