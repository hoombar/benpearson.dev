## What You'll Create

A spectacular erupting volcano on your kitchen table! 🌋 You'll shape a simple dough cone around a container, fill it with a secret mixture, then trigger a magnificent fizzing lava flow using a real acid-base chemical reaction — all from your kitchen cupboard.

## Have fun!

- 🔴 Try different food colouring combos — what does purple lava look like?
- ⏱️ Time how quickly the eruption starts after you drop in the baking soda
- 🔬 Repeat five times and write down what you observe each time
- 💡 Try more or less baking soda — does the eruption size change?

## Why It's Amazing

* **Chemistry:** Children see a real acid-base reaction — science made tangible and spectacular! 🧪
* **Scientific Method:** Varying the ingredients introduces hypothesis, experiment, and observation. 🔬
* **Maths:** Measuring spoonfuls and timing eruptions builds practical number sense. 📐
* **Curiosity:** The dramatic result motivates children to keep asking "why?" and experimenting. 🌟

## Pro Tips

For **ages 5–8**: Focus on the colours and the fizzing — no need to explain chemistry deeply. Let their curiosity lead the way.

For **ages 8–12**: Introduce the words "acid" and "base" and challenge them to predict the outcome before each eruption — then check if they were right.

Lay down newspaper or do this in the kitchen sink for easy clean-up!
