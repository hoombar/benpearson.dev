## What You'll Create

Grow something amazing! 🌱 Your young gardeners will learn to fold **newspaper** pages into sturdy little plant pots, fill them with soil, and plant **bean seeds** inside. The magic? When the seedlings are big enough, you plant the entire pot in the ground — the newspaper breaks down naturally in the soil! It's crafting, science, and gardening all in one brilliant activity.


## How to Set It Up

### Step 1: Fold the Pot Shape

Take a full sheet of **newspaper** and fold it in half lengthwise. Place a **glass jar** on its side at one end — the jar is your mould! Roll the newspaper tightly around the jar, leaving about 5cm overhanging the bottom. 📰


### Step 2: Form the Base

Fold the overhanging newspaper ends inward, tucking them over each other to create a flat base — like wrapping a present. Press it flat firmly. Carefully slide the jar out. You now have a newspaper pot that stands up on its own! Repeat to make several pots. 🏺


### Step 3: Fill and Plant

Stand your newspaper pots in a **baking tray** (to catch water). Fill each pot with soil or compost. Poke a hole about 2cm deep with your finger and drop in a **bean seed**. Cover with soil and give it a gentle sprinkle of **water**. 🌱


### Step 4: Watch Them Grow

Place your tray near a sunny window. Water gently every day or two — keep the soil moist but not soaked. In about a week you'll see green shoots poking through! When the seedlings are a few centimetres tall, plant the whole pot directly into the garden. 🌿


## Have fun!

- 📏 Measure your seedlings every day and make a growth chart!
- 🏷️ Make little plant labels from popsicle sticks and markers!
- 🧪 Experiment — does a pot by the window grow faster than one in a cupboard?
- 🌻 Try different seeds — cress, sunflower, or lettuce!


## Why It's Amazing

* **Plant Science:** Children witness the complete germination process — from seed to sprout — and learn what plants need to grow (water, warmth, light). 🔬

* **Sustainability:** Making pots from newspaper teaches upcycling and biodegradability — the pot returns to the earth! A tangible lesson in environmental responsibility. ♻️

* **Patience and Responsibility:** Daily watering and waiting for growth teaches patience, routine care, and the reward of nurturing something living. 🧠

* **Origami Skills:** The folding technique builds spatial reasoning and fine motor skills — each fold must be precise for the pot to hold its shape. ✋


## Pro Tips

For **ages 3–5**: Pre-fold the pots (the folding is tricky for small hands). Let them fill with soil and plant the seeds — getting hands dirty is the best part! Use fast-growing cress seeds for quicker results (sprouts in 3–4 days).

For **ages 5–8**: Show them the folding technique step by step. Bean seeds are perfect — they're big enough for small fingers and sprout dramatically. Start a growth diary with drawings of the plant each day.

For **ages 8–12**: Challenge them to master the folding independently and make pots of different sizes. Set up a proper experiment — same seeds, different conditions (light vs dark, warm vs cool, watered vs dry). Record and graph the results.

**Secret Pro Move**: Double-layer the newspaper by using two sheets together — the pot will be much sturdier and hold its shape better while the seedling grows. It still biodegrades perfectly when planted! 🎯
