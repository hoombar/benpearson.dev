## What You'll Create

Dem bones, dem bones! 💀 Your little anatomists will use different **pasta** shapes to build a brilliant skeleton on paper — **tube pasta** (penne or rigatoni) for the long bones, **uncooked spaghetti** for ribs and fingers, and other shapes for the skull, hips, and joints. Glue it all down with **PVA glue** and you have an amazing 3D anatomy art piece!

## Have fun!

- 🦕 Make a dinosaur skeleton instead — longer neck, big tail, tiny arms!
- 🎃 Paint the pasta white and the paper black for a spooky Halloween skeleton!
- 📏 Make a life-size skeleton using a huge roll of paper on the floor!
- 🔬 Look up a real skeleton diagram and see how accurate yours is!

## Why It's Amazing

* **Anatomy Introduction:** Children learn about the human skeleton — where bones are, what they look like, and how they connect. 🦴

* **Shape Recognition:** Matching pasta shapes to bone shapes requires comparing, categorising, and creative thinking. 🧠

* **Fine Motor Skills:** Placing small pasta pieces precisely and snapping spaghetti develops finger control and dexterity. ✋

* **Art & Science Fusion:** This activity beautifully combines scientific learning with creative art — STEAM at its best! 🎨

## Pro Tips

For **ages 3–5**: Pre-draw the outline and pre-sort the pasta. Let them glue the big pieces (legs, arms) while you handle the fiddly bits. Focus on the fun of building rather than anatomical accuracy.

For **ages 5–8**: Let them sort the pasta and decide which shapes go where. Introduce the names of major bones (skull, spine, ribs, femur). Challenge them to count how many ribs they give their skeleton.

For **ages 8–12**: Research the actual skeleton first and try for accuracy — 12 pairs of ribs, 5 lumbar vertebrae, etc. Introduce the concept of joints and how different pasta shapes can represent ball-and-socket vs. hinge joints. Make it anatomically impressive!

**Secret Pro Move**: Spray the finished skeleton with clear lacquer or hairspray — it seals the pasta in place permanently and gives it a lovely sheen. The skeleton will last for years! ✨
