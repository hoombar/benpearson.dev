## What You'll Create

Blast off to space! 🚀 Your young astronauts will build a rocket ship from a **cardboard tube**, complete with a pointed paper cone nose, triangular cardboard fins, and a blazing **tissue paper** flame bursting from the bottom. Paint it in bold space colours, add portholes and details, then hold it high and count down — 3, 2, 1, BLAST OFF!


## How to Set It Up

### Step 1: Paint the Rocket Body

Paint a **cardboard tube** with bright **poster paint** — silver, white, blue, or any rocket colour you like! Cover the whole tube evenly. Let it dry completely before adding details. A second coat makes it look even more impressive. 🎨


### Step 2: Make the Nose Cone

Cut a circle from **paper**, then cut a straight line from the edge to the centre. Overlap the edges and roll into a cone shape. Secure with **tape**. Fit it onto the top of the tube and tape it in place — your rocket now has a pointed nose! ⚠️ Adult Helper Needed for cutting. ✂️


### Step 3: Add Fins

Cut 3–4 small triangles from spare cardboard or thick **paper**. These are the rocket fins. Make a small fold along one edge of each triangle to create a tab. Tape or glue the tabs evenly around the bottom of the tube so the fins stick out. 🔺


### Step 4: Add Flames and Details

Cut strips of red, orange, and yellow **tissue paper**. Scrunch them slightly and tape them inside the bottom of the tube — these are the rocket flames! Add portholes with a **marker** (draw circles on the body), and decorate with stripes or a flag. 🔥


## Have fun!

- 🌟 Add stickers for stars and decorate the rocket with mission patches!
- 🧑‍🚀 Make a tiny astronaut from a peg or pipe cleaner to ride inside!
- 🌙 Create a moon and planet backdrop for your rocket to visit!
- 📖 Learn about real rockets — how do they work? What fuel do they use?


## Why It's Amazing

* **3D Construction:** Building a cone, attaching fins, and engineering a standing structure teaches fundamental construction and spatial reasoning. 🏗️

* **Space Science:** A gateway to learning about rockets, the solar system, astronauts, and the physics of thrust — igniting a love of STEM. 🚀

* **Fine Motor Skills:** Rolling cones, cutting triangles, folding tabs, and precise taping all build hand dexterity and coordination. ✋

* **Creative Expression:** From colour choices to porthole placement to flame design, every rocket is unique and personal. 🎨


## Pro Tips

For **ages 3–5**: Pre-cut the cone and fins. Let them paint the body and stuff the tissue paper flames inside. The countdown and "blast off" moment is what they'll remember!

For **ages 5–8**: Show them how to roll the cone and let them cut their own fins. Challenge them to make the fins evenly spaced. Add detailed decorations — portholes, flags, stripes.

For **ages 8–12**: Challenge them to build a multi-stage rocket by connecting two tubes together. Research real rocket designs (Saturn V, Falcon 9) and replicate the markings. Add a launch pad from a cardboard box.

**Secret Pro Move**: Wrap the tube in aluminium foil before decorating for a shiny metallic rocket body — it looks incredibly realistic and professional! 🎯
